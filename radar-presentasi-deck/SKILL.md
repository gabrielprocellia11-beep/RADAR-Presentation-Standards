# RAD+ar Proposal Deck & Diagram Standards

Learned from D:\000 RAD+AR\000 REFERENCES\002 PROPOSAL (44 decks, 2024–2026). The core rule: **every design idea must be explained by a diagram before it is shown as a render.** Diagrams tell the story, renders prove it, and mood images set the feeling.

## 1. Two deck formats

### A. Portfolio project sheet (5 slides, 16:9, 1440x810 pt)
Used for portfolio and competition entries (Carnivore, Observation Tower, Munduk, Pakuwon, Beanstar, HW Coffee, Megacosta and others).
1. **Cover**: title block on the left third, hero render on the right two-thirds.
2. **Concept / diagrams + exterior renders**: form-generation diagrams (small multiples) next to 1–2 large renders.
3. **Experience renders**: interior or eye-level renders as a collage (1 large + 2–3 small).
4. **Drawings**: plan(s) + section(s), often with zoning color, sometimes exploded axonometric or program stack.
5. **Atmosphere / closing**: render collage (night, detail, people, aerial).
Shorter projects compress this to 3–4 slides by merging slides 2 and 3.

### B. Full client proposal (25–65 slides)
Used for Void Uluwatu, Cylinders Bali Wellness, Bali Creative District, Pamulang Serang, Widya Dharma Husada, Green Silk Corridor.
1. **Cover**: white page, small line sketch or logo-mark of the project, then the line `CONCEPTUAL DESIGN / PROJECT NAME – OPTION X / LOCATION`, `RADAR` and the date. Minimal and centered.
2. **About RAD+ar**: a grid of past project thumbnails (one large photo of the office with the team) plus 3 paragraphs: who RAD+ar is, Tropical Architecture by Antonius Richard Rusli, globalization + localization.
3. **International recognition / published by**: logo wall (awards on top, media below).
4. **Disclaimer**: a centered text block saying it's a proposal or feasibility study and an intellectual property notice.
5. **Site / existing condition**: aerial drone photo of the site, then a photo with a massing overlay.
6. **Big idea / mood**: 1–3 reference images with a short caption naming the concept (e.g. "THE VOID – SKYSHAFT", "Poetic dynamic of space among grids").
7. **Site analysis diagrams**: existing axes, datum, perspective, grid, access, views, sun and wind (for Indonesian clients the text can be in Bahasa Indonesia).
8. **Form-generation diagram sequence**: one step per slide (see Section 3).
9. **Hero exterior render(s)**: full-bleed.
10. **Masterplan / site plan**: color-zoned, with a legend.
11. **Interior renders, each followed by its reference mood images**, alternating: render → mood board → render.
12. **Floor plans per level** with an area tabulation and a reference image strip.
13. **Sections / long section**, perspective section.
14. **Before / after aerial** (existing photo vs. proposal composited into the same photo).
15. **Materials / facade options**, then options or alternatives.
16. **Area tabulation table** (GFA, NLA, efficiency %, parking).
For several options in one deck: an option divider slide (`OPTION 2 – THE ARCHED COMMUNITY`), then repeat the diagram → mood → render loop for each option.

The pattern inside each design move is: **diagram → mood/reference images → render → (line-drawing version of the same render)**. In Pamulang Serang, every render is shown first as a pencil/line-art version and then as the full color render.

## 2. RADAR template layout rules
- White background. A thin `+` crop mark in all four corners.
- Right margin, vertical text: `www.radarchitecture.net` (top), **RADAR** logotype (middle, bold, rotated 90°), `@radarchitects` (bottom). Light grey, except RADAR, which is black.
- Alternative footer (A3 decks): centered `RADAR` with `@radarchitects` on the left and `www.radarchitecture.net` on the right, or bottom-left `RADAR  PROJECT NAME – LOCATION`.
- Images sit inside a generous margin (~4% left/right, ~8% top/bottom) and are never full-bleed on template slides. Full-bleed is reserved for hero renders and aerials in long decks.
- **Cover title block** (left third): PROJECT NAME in bold uppercase sans; then four lines `Location : City, Country` / `Typology : …` / `Gross Built Area : … / Undisclosed` / `Status : Conceptual / Proposal`; then at the bottom a justified English paragraph (~100–130 words) followed by the **Chinese translation** paragraph of the same text.
- Fonts: bold geometric sans for titles (Barlow ExtraBold / similar), Calibri or a light sans for body. Diagram titles are UPPERCASE, small, letter-spaced, with a one-line italic or light subtitle under them.
- Collage grids: one dominant image plus 2–3 smaller ones aligned to the same top and bottom edge, with small even gutters.

## 3. Diagram styles (pick the one that explains the design best)

| Style | When to use | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| **Form-generation sequence** (same view, one operation per frame: insert → cut → trim → interlock → light) | Showing how a sculptural form was derived | Cylinders Bali (CYLINDERS INSERTION, PARAMETRIC CUT, TRIMMED, INTERLOCK, LIGHT INSERTION) |
| **Exploded / axonometric site diagram with colored overlay** on white line context | Site logic: datum, axes, grid, floor extension, voids | Void Uluwatu (EXISTING AND DATUM, POWER OF PERSPECTIVE, GRID, FLOOR–FUNCTION–EXTENSION, VOID INTERSECTION) |
| **Hand-drawn marker sketch** (black line, grey mass, green trees, one accent color, handwritten labels) | Early concept and parameters, which reads as approachable for the client | Widya Dharma Husada (height parameters, grid module, floating pavilion, connection layer) and Green Silk Corridor (pink massing) |
| **Small-multiples massing 2x3 / 3x2 grid** in a soft rendered axo with arrows | Step-by-step massing on terrain | Carnivore, Megacosta Verdan, Riau, Omah Batu |
| **Bubble / zoning section** (colored blocks per function, handwritten labels) | Program stacking | Widya Dharma spatial zoning, Megacosta MSI |
| **Colored-floor section / elevation** (pink, purple and green bands with level markers) | Vertical program and levels | Widya Dharma, Pakuwon |
| **Exploded program axonometric** (floors pulled apart, each tinted) | Mixed-use stacking | Pakuwon (Hotel / Mall / Basement), Megacosta MSI |
| **Annotated render** (greyscale render with boxed labels and dashed leaders) | Pointing out functions on the real image | Widya Dharma p10/p37 |
| **Narrative axo with speech bubbles + icons** ($, growth chart) | Business case for developers | Pamulang Serang (Mall as visitor magnet, economic driver, property-value catalyst) |
| **Perspective / long section with people and light** | Spatial experience and daylight | Carnivore (animal ↔ diner section), Cylinders light modules, Observation Tower |
| **Icon / silhouette diagram** (solid black shapes) | One simple idea: arch, grid, skylight permutations | Pamulang arches, Void skylight permutations, grid → twisted grid |
| **Plan diagrams in sequence** (1. extend garden 2. create gathering 3. integrate infra 4. connect public) | Landscape and public-space strategy | Pamulang Serang |
| **Color-zoned floor plans** (orange retail, pink F&B, blue, green) + reference photo strip on the right | Tenant mix per floor | Green Silk Corridor p36–43 |
| **Watercolor / soft-wash context** (pale greens, misty trees) behind crisp object | Tropical, landscape-led projects | Cylinders Bali, Munduk |

General diagram rules:
- Context in light grey or white line; the design itself in the one or two accent colors. Red or terracotta for people and key moves; blue for water, voids and sky; green for landscape.
- Every diagram gets a title (UPPERCASE) and a one-line explanation of what the move does.
- Show human figures (often red) for scale in sections.
- Keep the same camera angle across a sequence so only the design change moves.
- Bilingual captions (EN + 中文) for China-facing decks; EN + Bahasa for Indonesian developers.

## 4. Mood / reference image slides
- 2–4 photos in a tight collage on white, with a small bold caption block naming the space (e.g. "DOUBLE-VOLUME FARMERS MARKET", "FLOATING – IDENTITY TENANTS", "SKY OFFICE") plus 2–3 lines of description.
- Place a mood slide directly before the render that realises it.
- Benchmarks (precedent malls etc.) get a structured card: name, architect, year, area, concept + 2–3 photos (Green Silk Corridor p26–31).

## 5. Building a deck as PPT
- 16:9. Portfolio sheets are 1440x810 pt (13.333 x 7.5 in); A3-landscape decks are also used.
- Put the RADAR margin elements (corner crosses, vertical RADAR, URL, handle) on the slide master so every slide inherits them.
- Layouts to define: Cover-Title-Block, Full-Bleed Render, 1-Large + 2-Small Collage, 2x3 Diagram Grid, Diagram + Caption, Plan + Legend, Mood Collage + Caption, Option Divider, Table.
- When explaining a new design, plan the diagram list first (site → concept → form generation → program → circulation → section/light), then attach renders and moods to each diagram.

## 6. Lessons from competition winners (TerraViva, Buildner / competitions.archi)
Taken from the 2026 winning boards (Ruinas de Quilmes, Call of the Sea, Nidos de Agua, Karlovo Living Landscape, Museum of Emotions #8). Competition boards are A1/A2 landscape and very dense, so the lessons below are about clarity. They apply to RAD+ar slides directly.

### Diagram types worth adding to RAD+ar decks
- **Numbered process strip (01–05).** Five small isometrics of the same block in one row, a single red or terracotta accent for the move in each, and a 2–3 line caption under each number (compact volume → path splits it → terrain bends → ramps extended → roof becomes landscape). A second row abstracts the same idea as pure line gestures. *Chakana, Ruinas de Quilmes 1st.*
- **Concept vignette grid.** Small square plan diagrams in one earthy fill colour with arrows, each with a serif title: Carving Out Voids, Programmatic Fluidity, Unprescribed Ground, Traffic Strategy. Next to them sits one exploded **spatial layering** axo (roof / landscape / ground floor stacked with dashed leaders). *Pirca Aflora.*
- **Element catalogue.** A row of identical-size isometric "cut blocks" (a cube of ground with one element on it: totem, steps, bench, seating, pergola), each with a small symbol + name. It explains a kit of parts that repeats across the site.
- **Exploded isometric with flow line.** The building pulled apart vertically, one blue line tracing water (or air, light, people) through every layer, and side notes with tiny icons on dashed leaders. *The Rainwell, Call of the Sea.*
- **Exploded construction axo.** Roof, structure, living level and deck separated, with labels on the left aligned to each layer. *The New Collective, Call of the Sea 1st.*
- **Sketch-to-story header.** A loose hand sketch of the one idea under the project title, then a short narrative, then a mini **timeline infographic** of the problem (dates + silhouettes). This sells *why* before *what*.
- **Section series with figures.** Six small sections in a 2x3 grid, each labelled by activity (Fish market, Workshop, Informal social space), with people and boats for scale.
- **Strategy icon row.** Four small diagrams of the same site plan across the top of a board: Harmonizing with terrain / Preserving density / Programmatic zoning / Solar strategy, each with one colour. *Karlovo 1st.*
- **Phasing triptych.** The same white clay massing model, three times: phase 1, 2, 3.
- **Circular / route-following layout.** The board's own composition follows the plan geometry (a ring project laid out in a ring). Buildner's jury praised this.

### Rules the winners follow
- **One accent colour per diagram set.** Context is grey line or white clay. The move is red, terracotta, blue (water) or green (landscape), never more than two.
- **Same viewpoint, same scale, same size** across a series, so only the design change moves.
- **Numbers + 1-line captions** turn diagrams into a readable sequence; titles are short nouns or verbs ("Carving Out Voids").
- **Line drawings stay light.** Thin, grey hatch for terrain, black poché only for the cut. Renders carry the emotion; diagrams carry the logic.
- **Hero render dominant** (about 40–50% of the first board), with diagrams and text in a narrow column beside it.
- **Locally rooted materials and people** in every render (boats, villagers, local timber), which matters for RAD+ar's tropical Indonesian work.

### How Buildner judges presentations (use as a checklist)
They score each entry on: Linework, Quality of drawings, Balance of colour, Layout, Hierarchy, Annotation, Text, Clarity of story, Clarity of diagrams, Overall quality (each /10). Their recurring critiques:
- Strong mood images aren't enough. They lose points when there are too few architectural drawings to show the entrance, scale and transitions.
- Inconsistent or AI-generated views that don't match each other reduce credibility; keep renders consistent with plans and sections.
- The best boards make the central spatial idea understandable within seconds, through a clear hierarchy and a restrained palette.
- A single exploded diagram or section that shows *how the idea works* (e.g. a shared membrane between two floors) scores higher than many decorative views.

Before finishing any RAD+ar deck, check each design move: does it have (1) a diagram that explains it, (2) a drawing that proves its scale and access, and (3) a render or mood image that sells the feeling?

## 7. Tambahan aturan kerja (dari latihan)
- Semua slide/papan dibuat sebagai file berlayer (PSD/AI, atau deck yang bisa diedit) — bukan gambar datar.
- Gambar dasar diekspor dari model 3D (lihat `radar-sumber-3d`), tidak memakai screenshot user.
- Setiap denah, tampak, potongan, dan axo diberi lanskap + orang berskala (lihat `radar-entourage`).
- Denah berwarna per lantai mengikuti `radar-warna-denah`; diagram mengikuti `radar-diagram-konsep`.
